# Coping and work-stress reference notes

Bibliographic records and primary-study abstracts checked on 2026-10-29. Full texts were not reviewed. These sources are not labeled UTD24 papers.

## Susan Folkman, Richard S. Lazarus, Christine Dunkel-Schetter, Anita DeLongis, and Rand J. Gruen (1986)

Dynamics of a stressful encounter: Cognitive appraisal, coping, and encounter outcomes. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 50(5), 992–1003.

https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.50.5.992

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3712234/

**Supports:** Appraisal, coping options and forms of coping were associated within individuals across stressful encounters.

**Does not establish:** Observational associations do not identify the best randomized support sequence or prove a controllability-by-treatment causal interaction.

**Proposed design use:** Motivate measured controllability as a candidate moderator; the intervention-matching hypothesis is a proposed extension.

## Susan Folkman and Richard S. Lazarus (1985)

If it changes it must be a process: Study of emotion and coping during three stages of a college examination. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 48(1), 150–170.

https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.48.1.150

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2980281/

**Supports:** Coping and emotions changed across exam stages; participants used multiple forms of problem- and emotion-focused coping.

**Does not establish:** An examination study is not a workplace SMART and does not establish that combining assigned supports is superior.

**Proposed design use:** Motivate sequence-sensitive hypotheses. The claim is not that earlier coping theory was static; this tool supports experimental tests of dynamic support rules.

## Evangelia Demerouti, Arnold B. Bakker, Friedhelm Nachreiner, and Wilmar B. Schaufeli (2001)

The job demands-resources model of burnout. *Journal of Applied Psychology*, 86(3), 499–512.

https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-9010.86.3.499

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11419809/

**Supports:** Work demands and resources related differently to exhaustion and disengagement across occupational groups.

**Does not establish:** This study does not validate the demo stress score or establish effects of the two proposed coping programs.

**Proposed design use:** Situate stress in work conditions; demands, resources and burnout would require separate measures for a full JD-R test.

## Sabine Sonnentag and Charlotte Fritz (2007)

The Recovery Experience Questionnaire: Development and validation of a measure for assessing recuperation and unwinding from work. *Journal of Occupational Health Psychology*, 12(3), 204–221.

https://doi.org/10.1037/1076-8998.12.3.204

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17638488/

**Supports:** Four recovery experiences were distinguished: detachment, relaxation, mastery and control.

**Does not establish:** Recovery experiences are not interchangeable with coping strategies, stress scores or the demo controllability moderator.

**Proposed design use:** Guide future measurement of recovery as a distinct outcome or mechanism; this release simulates stress, not the Recovery Experience Questionnaire.
